# Academic Review Language Rules

## Replace Vague Packaging With Technical Objects

Avoid:

- "technical chain"
- "pain points"
- "closed loop"
- "intelligent trend"
- "AI-generated workflow"
- "the discussion covers A, B, C, D"
- "it is crucial to note"

Prefer:

- joint arrangement,
- anisotropic friction,
- centerline discretization,
- contact force term,
- look-ahead distance,
- safety-distance constraint,
- state estimation error,
- repeat success rate,
- model parameter drift.

## Strong Academic Sentence Pattern

Use:

> Method X maps state A to control variable B under constraint C; it is therefore suitable for scene D, but fails when condition E changes.

Avoid:

> Method X has broad prospects and can improve system performance in complex environments.

## Citation-Aware Claims

When citing a source, connect it to a specific role:

- "used for platform evidence",
- "used for the friction model",
- "used for the path tracking formulation",
- "used for experimental sequence comparison",
- "used as a structural reference."

Do not cite a review paper repeatedly for claims that should be supported by the original technical work.

## Slide Wording

Good slide titles:

- `Direction-dependent resistance and propulsion conditions`
- `From continuous body curve to discrete joint commands`
- `Contact force terms in multi-body dynamics`
- `LOS guidance and whole-body safety constraints`

Weak slide titles:

- `Motion Control`
- `Research Hotspots`
- `Technology Route`
- `Problem Analysis`

## De-AI Checklist

Search final text for:

- process notes,
- inflated adjectives,
- unsupported "latest" claims,
- repeated transition phrases,
- long list sentences,
- claims not tied to variables, figures, formulas, or metrics.

Then rewrite each flagged sentence around a specific object, relation, or evidence source.
