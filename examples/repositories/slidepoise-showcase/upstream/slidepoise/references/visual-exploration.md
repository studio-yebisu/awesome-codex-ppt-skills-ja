# Explore visuals that explain

Use this guidance when developing or reconsidering a slide's visual approach. Attractive composition should earn attention and help the audience understand the information. A clean arrangement of text can still leave all the explanatory work to the reader.

## Start with the intended understanding

Identify what the reader needs to notice, compare, trace or infer. Keep that relationship separate from the arrangement of objects. An outline with three topics establishes content, not three equal columns. A source table establishes facts, not necessarily the final visual form.

Explore plausible ways to make the important relationship visible before committing the image-generation brief. Use inspected references, subject assets, rough sketches or a deliberately different generated candidate when they resolve a real design question. Alternatives should change how the reader understands the material. Recolouring the same grid is not a different visual idea. The amount of exploration depends on uncertainty and the brief. There is no required count of concepts, images or layouts.

Search beyond the first familiar treatment when candidates only package the outline. Change the reference query toward the relationship or reader task, inspect neighbouring pages, or retrieve relevant real-world objects and identity assets. A named provider or an unused library does not establish that this research happened.

Record the useful alternatives, inspected evidence and choice briefly in the existing resource `selection_reasoning.visual_approach`. Put only the chosen direction and remaining freedom into `style_direction`. Keep rejected options and tentative exclusions out of the generation instructions. An Agent proposal becomes a design choice through this reasoning. It never becomes an explicit user requirement.

## Use principles as judgement aids

For an experience involving several participants, reason through their different goals, acceptable tradeoffs and resulting benefits before drawing a journey. Name each handoff's initiator, recipient and response. A list of stages or named people alone does not explain their interaction. Consider a participant interaction map or service blueprint when responsibilities cross, without prescribing lanes or a fixed number of stages. Preserve the motivations and reciprocal effects in the visible page, not only in its speaker notes.

Choose the level of explanation before selecting a story. A product operating flow explains stakeholder roles, responsibilities and interactions across the service. A worked example illustrates one instance of that flow. Do not substitute named people, sample purchases or illustrative amounts for a requested general process. Concrete language can describe the actual work without introducing an example.

- **Make the relationship perceptible.** Position, grouping, paths, size and contrast can reveal how information relates. Choose the form around the conclusion the audience should reach. Keep data scales and implied relationships truthful. A table earns its place when the reader needs aligned comparison or exact lookup. A diagram earns its place when its spatial relationships explain something the text alone makes harder to follow. [Duarte's guidance on displaying data](https://www.duarte.com/blog/display-data-in-presentations/) connects visual choice to the relationship being emphasised and distinguishes spoken presentations from self-contained reading.
- **Direct attention deliberately.** Give the consequential point visual emphasis and let context support it. Decluttering alone can produce a tidy page with no focus. Retain evidence and qualifications that the claim needs, and place labels close to what they explain. [Storytelling with Data](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/two-tips-for-better-graphs) demonstrates the separate contributions of removing distractions and focusing attention.
- **Balance meaning as well as space.** Scale and contrast signal priority. Proximity signals association. Equal weight suggests peer importance, so symmetry can be misleading when the argument has a clear cause, exception or conclusion. Balance can be asymmetrical. [Nielsen Norman Group's visual-design principles](https://media.nngroup.com/media/articles/attachments/Principles_Visual_Design-A4.pdf) describe these perceptual relationships. Their numerical rules of thumb are contextual examples, not quotas for this skill.
- **Make the idea specific.** Exact identities, authentic evidence, familiar objects or a well-chosen metaphor can improve recognition and memory. Judge what each adds to this subject. A stock symbol for innovation or a decorative network can be as formulaic as a repeated card grid. [Duarte's critique of visual clichés](https://www.duarte.com/blog/the-cliche/) encourages exploring beyond the first familiar association.

These principles guide choices. They do not prescribe slide types, a fixed reading pattern or a mandatory central illustration. Preserve the user's actual layout requirements when provided.

## Explain architecture at the useful level

An architecture slide may need to explain both how a system works and why its boundaries or controls matter. Decide which question the audience needs answered before adding component detail. Make ownership, flows and shared dependencies visible. Describe each consequential boundary or handoff in language a reader can understand without knowing the implementation.

When explanations would crowd the diagram, consider short numbered markers linked to a nearby explanatory rail. Use them for design principles, key interactions or selected steps, according to the content. Numbers used as cross-references do not imply chronology. Let the number of markers, their placement and the space for explanation follow the architecture and reading task. A conventional legend may be enough. Preserve direction and meaning, including returns and exception paths that matter to the claim.

Keep an optional user suggestion optional while exploring. A reference with numbered notes does not require that count, a fixed side rail or a particular diagram-to-text ratio. Transfer its useful reasoning, such as ownership boundaries or controlled handoffs, without inheriting its outer skeleton. If notes repeat paragraphs already inside nodes, revise the information allocation before reconstruction. Judge the resulting image independently of the reference's reputation.

## Use image generation for composition

Give the generator the message, evidence, semantic relationships, useful assets and relevant references, with room to compose. Check `visual_obligations`, `avoid`, shared guidance and `style_direction` for Agent-authored layout restrictions before compiling. Retain restrictions that protect meaning or implement a reasoned choice. Remove inherited habits and convenience rules such as banning logos or requiring equal panels without a page-specific basis.

Do not choose a table, card grid or all-text treatment merely because it will be easier to reconstruct. The editable pipeline serves the selected communication design. When the first image only boxes paragraphs, or its metaphor needs an explanation of its own, revisit the approach before spending effort on reconstruction. A focused edit suits a local defect. A weak explanatory structure calls for another composition.

Separate a design judgement from an asset-production problem. If a generated visual clarifies the subject, an absent library match is a reason to source, extract or refine the asset. It is not by itself a reason to discard the design or replace the visual with prose. Inspect a faithful library candidate or the original crop at its intended size, then use the raster-composition and illustration-refinement routes when they preserve the selected visual better. Explain any genuine limitation. A user-selected visual treatment remains the target while its production is resolved.

Review the page at reading size and alongside the deck. Identify the first thing a cold reader sees and the relationship the visual actually explains. If removing the visual leaves comprehension unchanged, consider whether it needs a clearer purpose. Repeated structures are useful for deliberate comparison, while repeated structures across unrelated arguments can flatten emphasis. Seek coherent typography, colour and identity without imposing the same skeleton on every page.
