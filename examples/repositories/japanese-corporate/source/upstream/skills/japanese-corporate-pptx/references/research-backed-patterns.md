## What repeats across real examples

### 1. The page closes a discussion

The slide does not only announce a theme. It normally contains:

- the issue or conclusion;
- the system, process, timeline, or stakeholder model;
- evidence or concrete examples;
- conditions and exceptions;
- the implication or requested decision;
- source and date.

### 2. One dominant model carries many annotations

Good traditional pages are dense because many labels explain one system. They are not a collection of unrelated cards.

Use:

- one timeline with multiple swimlanes;
- one center-and-surround ecosystem;
- one process with role lanes;
- one layer stack with side enablers and constraints;
- one policy scheme with purpose, actors, flow, and outcome.

### 3. Reading order is imposed with rails and color

Typical order:

1. dark blue topic rail;
2. black or blue conclusion title;
3. dominant figure;
4. cyan or green structural highlights;
5. red current-point, risk, or decision annotation;
6. gray notes and source.

### 4. Small text has a defined role

Compact text is acceptable for:

- source and caveat;
- evidence details;
- actor responsibilities;
- examples inside a larger model;
- dates, units, and definitions.

Do not use compact text for the primary claim, entity labels, or required action.

### 5. Images are framed, not decorative

Japanese corporate examples usually treat images as:

- a documentary record with caption;
- an official chart or diagram excerpt with source;
- a facility, product, or process reference;
- a portrait tied to identity and role.

The image normally sits inside the information structure. It does not become an unrelated full-screen hero.

### 6. Working papers expose document state

Common controls include:

- material number or agenda position;
- meeting or committee name;
- publication or reference date;
- responsible division;
- `案`, `暫定`, `一部加工`, `要確認`, or `継続審議`;
- page-level source and processing notes.

These labels are used only when they are accurate.

### 7. Source excerpts are interpreted

A useful excerpt page includes:

- a bounded crop;
- a red focus box or arrow;
- caption and source page;
- an accurate `抜粋` or `一部加工` note;
- comparison, implication, and decision.

For a single chart that carries the page:

- give the chart a dominant evidence zone without copying a source page's exact dimensions;
- place the conclusion in a separate top, side, or bottom zone chosen for the new story;
- use red dotted vertical markers only for real dates, thresholds, or decision points;
- separate causal interpretation from the original plot, such as in a side reading rail;
- keep the original title, axis, legend, units, and source visible;
- state whether the source was cropped, translated, highlighted, or otherwise processed;
- preserve the source chart as an image when the data cannot be reproduced faithfully.

## Copyright and attribution

- Use public examples as visual research unless reuse rights are clear.
- Do not bundle public screenshots into a reusable template.
- When an official figure is necessary evidence, use a bounded source excerpt and cite the organization, document title, page, date, and URL.
- Prefer recreating the relationship model with native editable shapes when the content itself is not copyrighted or is user-owned.
- Do not trace one reference's distinctive page composition. Retain the diagram grammar while changing at least three of macro layout, reading order, emphasis position, annotation geometry, color roles, and implication placement.
- Do not recreate a chart from appearance alone. Use verified underlying data or preserve the attributed source chart as an excerpt.
