# Writing and Density

## Copy style

Write formal, direct Japanese suitable for a committee, manufacturing company, ministry, or executive meeting.

Prefer:

- explicit actors and actions;
- cause and effect;
- dates, units, boundaries, and definitions;
- visible conditions and exceptions;
- conclusion-bearing titles;
- short labels inside diagrams;
- longer explanation only in a dedicated evidence or note zone.

Avoid:

- abstract slogan chains;
- startup language;
- unnecessary katakana;
- repeated noun phrases;
- hidden actors;
- generic labels such as `価値向上` without saying whose value and how.

## Title hierarchy

Use:

1. top rail for the topic or section;
2. main title for the claim, model, or decision;
3. optional subtitle for scope, definition, or reading instruction.

Example:

- Rail: `生成AI活用の全体像`
- Main title: `社内データ・モデル・業務を分離し、統制点を置く`
- Subtitle: `利用者からインフラまでの5層で責任範囲を整理`

Do not let a rail title or primary title wrap unexpectedly.

## Density budget

Traditional Japanese corporate slides may use 75–90% of the canvas.

Use these tiers:

- 1 primary conclusion;
- 1 dominant diagram or evidence composition;
- 4–8 major entities or stages;
- 6–14 supporting labels;
- 2–5 evidence, exception, or implication notes;
- 1 source line.

More blocks are acceptable when all blocks belong to the same model. Fewer blocks are better when the topic is simple.

Do not force a dense page into three independent cards.

## Text tiers

- Primary message: one to three short lines.
- Entity label: noun phrase plus role.
- Relationship label: what moves, changes, or is decided.
- Supporting note: fact, example, boundary, or implication.
- Source note: organization, document, page, date, and URL when appropriate.

Compact supporting text must not compete visually with the conclusion.

## Diagram writing

Label arrows with concrete relationships:

- `データ連携`
- `設計結果をフィードバック`
- `委託費`
- `承認`
- `製品・サービス`
- `運用ログ`

Avoid unlabeled arrows when the meaning is not obvious.

For each actor, state at least one of:

- responsibility;
- input;
- output;
- decision;
- risk;
- benefit.

## One-slide closure

A serious one-page ポンチ normally answers:

1. What is the issue or conclusion?
2. What is the whole system, process, or timeline?
3. Who is involved?
4. What moves between them?
5. What evidence supports the model?
6. What conditions or exceptions matter?
7. What decision or action follows?

If the page cannot answer the relevant questions without oral explanation, it is incomplete.

## Numbers

- Use `6,000名以上`, not `6,000+`.
- Use `100社以上`, not `100社+`.
- Use `2,000万円`, not `¥20M`.
- Show unit, period, actual/forecast/estimate status, and source.
- Keep significant digits consistent.

## Sources and caveats

Use compact, factual wording:

`出所：経済産業省「製造業を巡る現状と課題 今後の政策の方向性」（2024年5月）、p.32`

`注：数値は公開情報を基にした概算。条件により変動。`

`注：本図は関係者間の役割を説明するための模式図。実際の契約関係とは異なる。`

`参考：画面・図表は説明用に一部を抜粋。`

For annotated source charts, separate three voices:

- source voice — the original chart title, axes, legend, units, and labels;
- analyst voice — added conclusion, time markers, and causal interpretation;
- decision voice — the implication or action stated below the chart.

Do not silently rewrite source labels. If Japanese translation, cropping, highlighting, or rearrangement was added, say so in the processing note.

Working-document status labels have specific meanings:

- `本日決定` — the meeting is expected to decide the item;
- `要確認` — a named fact or dependency still needs verification;
- `継続審議` — the decision remains open after this meeting;
- `暫定` — the value or structure is temporary;
- `一部加工` — the source figure has actually been cropped, emphasized, translated, or rearranged.

Use the label only when that state is true.

## Profile and company slides

Do not write only a biography.

Combine:

- current role;
- relevant history;
- business portfolio;
- evidence or proof points;
- one to three documentary images;
- relationship to the current topic.

Use only roles confirmed by the user or source material. Do not default to
`メイン講師`, `登壇者`, `責任者`, or another delivery role unless that person
actually holds the role for the specific engagement.
