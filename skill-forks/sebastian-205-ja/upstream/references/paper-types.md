# Paper-Type Routes

Select the closest primary type from the paper's central contribution, not its journal, field label, or most visually prominent figure. Combine routes only when the paper has two genuinely co-equal contributions. Do not force any paper into a fixed slide template; use the route to order claims and evidence.

## Discovery and mechanism

**Arc:** unresolved phenomenon -> question or hypothesis -> experimental system -> primary observation -> perturbation or causal evidence -> orthogonal validation -> bounded mechanism -> implications and open questions.

**Evidence checks:**

- Distinguish association from causal perturbation.
- Preserve controls, conditions, timing, dose, scale bars, and statistical qualifiers.
- Verify that each mechanistic arrow is directly supported; label author models and your synthesis.
- Avoid presenting a summary model as measured evidence.

## Clinical, population, and intervention studies

**Arc:** clinical or population need -> study question -> design and population -> exposure or intervention -> primary endpoint -> effect and uncertainty -> safety or secondary outcomes -> bias and generalizability -> implications.

**Evidence checks:**

- Verify study design, recruitment, inclusion criteria, sample sizes, comparator, endpoint definitions, and follow-up.
- Report effect size with confidence intervals or uncertainty, not significance alone.
- Separate prespecified, subgroup, exploratory, and observational findings.
- Avoid causal language when the design supports association only.

## Methods, AI, and tools

**Arc:** task and bottleneck -> proposed method -> inputs and workflow -> evaluation design -> main benchmark -> robustness or ablation -> failure analysis -> practical value and limits.

**Evidence checks:**

- Verify datasets, splits, baselines, metrics, leakage controls, and external validation.
- Compare like with like and retain uncertainty or repeated-run information.
- Distinguish architecture description from demonstrated advantage.
- Show failure modes, resource costs, and operating assumptions when reported.

## Resources, datasets, omics, and benchmarks

**Arc:** unmet resource need -> collection or cohort -> generation and quality control -> organization or atlas -> validation -> access and reuse -> coverage, bias, and limitations.

**Evidence checks:**

- Verify sample provenance, scale, modalities, processing, QC, annotation, and access conditions.
- Distinguish resource construction from discoveries made with the resource.
- Preserve batch, coverage, missingness, and representativeness caveats.
- Explain benchmark tasks and evaluation boundaries before ranking methods.

## Materials, chemistry, physics, and engineering

**Arc:** performance or scientific constraint -> design principle -> synthesis, fabrication, or setup -> characterization -> primary performance -> comparison and trade-offs -> mechanism or model -> durability, scalability, and limits.

**Evidence checks:**

- Preserve operating conditions, units, normalization, uncertainty, and reference baselines.
- Verify that cross-paper comparisons use compatible protocols and definitions.
- Separate measured behavior, fitted models, simulations, and proposed mechanisms.
- Include stability, repeatability, manufacturability, or scale limits when central to the claim.

## Reviews, perspectives, and systematic reviews

**Arc:** field question -> scope and selection method -> evidence landscape -> areas of agreement -> contradictions or gaps -> organizing framework -> implications and research agenda.

**Evidence checks:**

- For systematic reviews, verify search scope, eligibility, study count, bias assessment, synthesis method, and heterogeneity.
- For narrative reviews or perspectives, distinguish cited evidence from author opinion.
- Avoid turning a conceptual taxonomy into empirical proof.
- Preserve uncertainty, disagreement, and publication or selection bias.

## Final selection check

Before committing to an arc, state the paper's primary contribution in one sentence and name the evidence that establishes it. If the sentence describes a method but the decisive evidence is a clinical endpoint, or describes a mechanism but only correlations are available, revise the classification or narrow the claim. Adapt slide allocation to the available evidence rather than padding every route to the same length.
