# Figure, Formula, And Table Guidelines

## Figure Explanation Notes

Use visible explanation notes when a slide contains a conceptually important image. Keep the note short enough to be read in five seconds.

Recommended labels:

- `Read the figure`: for images, mechanisms, experiments, and diagrams.
- `Read the table`: for comparison matrices and classification pages.
- `Formula meaning`: for equations, variables, and model terms.

For Chinese academic slides, use:

- `读图要点`
- `读表要点`
- `公式含义`

## What To Explain

For a literature figure:

- what object or experiment is shown,
- which part of the image supports the slide claim,
- what the audience should compare across subfigures,
- what source the figure comes from.

For a formula:

- what each variable or term represents,
- what physical or algorithmic relationship the formula encodes,
- where the formula enters the workflow,
- what assumption limits its use.

For a table:

- what the rows and columns classify,
- which comparison dimension matters most,
- why this table is not merely a list.

## Formula Handling

Do not paste corrupt equations into PPT. If MathType or LaTeX rendering is unavailable, use a formula placeholder plus a plain-language explanation.

Acceptable placeholder:

> Formula position: multi-body dynamics equation. Source: Author Year. Explain inertia, actuation, contact constraint, and external resistance terms.

For final slide decks, prefer:

- LaTeX-rendered equations as high-resolution vector or PNG when formulas are stable.
- PPT native text for variable explanations.
- A short `Formula meaning` box when the audience needs the equation but not the full derivation.

## Captions And Source Notes

Use compact captions:

`Pipeline inspection sequence [Liu 2024]`

or in Chinese:

`管道检测实验序列 [Liu 2024]`

Do not cite "paper folder", "user source", or "screenshot source" in the final artifact.

## Visual QA Checklist

- No label is smaller than the smallest body text unless it is an original figure label.
- A figure caption does not collide with image borders.
- Formula notes do not cover the equation.
- Generated illustrations contain no unverified text.
- Readability survives PNG export at presentation resolution.
